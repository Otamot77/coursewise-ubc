How the percentile estimate works and what it can't tell you

For unlocked courses with fully published historical grade bands, Coursewise finds a common within-course percentile whose estimated grades reach your credit-weighted target. The estimates use linear interpolation inside each published grade range, so they're inherently approximate, especially for the broad 0–49 and 90–100 bands. A percentile of 60 means roughly the 60th percentile in each course, not a 60% probability of reaching your target or an observed semester-wide student ranking.

The data contains no matched individual students across courses. Missing or privacy-suppressed bands, incomplete sections, and missing credit values are never silently filled in. Course baseline means may come from different academic sessions. Planner settings stay in this browser only; they aren't sent to the server.

UBC records percentage and letter grades; the 4.33 table is an illustrative equivalency. Different institutions may convert grades differently. Fractional historical averages are rounded to the nearest whole percentage point for the lookup and marked ≈; converting a course's average is not the same as averaging every student's GPA. The published UBC equivalency table doesn't list grades below 50%, so those are shown as F without an invented numeric equivalency. Sources: UBC Faculty of Arts ↗ and UBC Student Services ↗.

04 / TRANSPARENCY

Better insights. Clearer sources.

Knowing what the data can't tell you is part of making a good decision.

01

Real grade data, not guesses.

UBC originally published the historical grades through PAIR reports and grade-distribution dashboards. Your local index is built from CSVs preserved in DonneyF’s community-maintained archive, not a live connection to UBC. If the local index is missing a course, Coursewise may call the UBCGrades API instead.

02

Mind the missing grades.

UBC's reports suppress some small grade categories for privacy. We leave those blank rather than reverse-engineering suppressed student counts.

03

Historical ≠ predictive.

Past grades vary by instructor, section and year. Use the results as context—not as a guaranteed grade or a complete difficulty score.
